## Executive Summary of Issues

This document registers all identified bugs, methodological problems, experimental discrepancies, and critical peer-reviewer vulnerabilities in the current repository. Issues are categorized by severity:

- **Severity 1 (Critical / Showstopper):** Factual contradictions or methodological flaws that would lead to immediate rejection or paper retraction if unaddressed.
- **Severity 2 (High / Reviewer Vulnerability):** Methodological limitations or missing baselines that a skeptical reviewer will aggressively attack.
- **Severity 3 (Medium / Experimental Gap):** Incomplete multi-seed coverage, missing comparison figures, or incomplete artifact generation scripts.
- **Severity 4 (Low / Cosmetic & Documentation):** Inconsistencies in documentation, markdown tables, or orphaned drafts.

## Severity 1: Critical Factual & Experimental Contradictions

### Issue 1.1: Severe README vs. Code Discrepancy in Threat Model Configuration
- **Location:** `README.md` (lines 147, 152, 155) vs. `configs/robust_federated_config.json` (lines 13, 21), `scratch/run_robust_experiments.py` (lines 160-165), and `results/reports/robust_federated_report.md` (lines 64-65).
- **Description:**
  - `README.md` explicitly states:
    > "Adversary Budget: 2 malicious clients ($f=2$, 20% of fleet), specifically designated as `client_0` and `client_1`... Logged: `client_0` flipped 1,845 labels (100%), `client_1` flipped 2,427 labels (100%)... In our benchmark, $\gamma = 1.5$."
  - In reality, the configuration file, python execution script, and experimental reports reveal:
    > Malicious clients are `[8, 9]`!
    > `client_8` holds 3,572 samples and `client_9` holds 5,101 samples (totaling 8,673 samples, or **39.65%** of the entire training dataset on Seed 42).
    > Scaling factor $\gamma = 1.0$ (not 1.5).
- **Scientific Impact:** 
  In sample-weighted FedAvg, the impact of an adversary is proportional to their sample weight. Having 2 clients with 39.65% data weight is vastly more damaging than having 2 clients with 19.5% data weight (`client_0` and `client_1` hold 1,296 and 1,347 samples = 2,643 / 21,875 = 12.08%). Claiming clients `[0, 1]` with $\gamma=1.5$ while evaluating `[8, 9]` with $\gamma=1.0$ is an egregious factual contradiction.
- **Resolution Required:** Update `README.md` and paper methodology to match the exact evaluated configuration: malicious client IDs `[8, 9]`, scaling factor $\gamma = 1.0$, and total poisoned sample counts.

## Severity 2: Methodological Vulnerabilities (Skeptical Reviewer Attacks)

### Issue 2.1: Single-Vehicle Dataset Used to Simulate Fleet Federated Learning
- **Vulnerability:** The HCRL Car-Hacking Dataset was recorded from a single commercial vehicle: an ambient Kia Soul. All 5 scenarios (Normal, DoS, Fuzzy, Gear, RPM) originate from the identical vehicle architecture, identical CAN bus speed (500 kbps), and identical ECU message IDs.
- **Reviewer Objection:** *"How can you claim to evaluate cross-vehicle federated learning when all simulated clients are generated by slicing a single car's CAN trace? In reality, different vehicle makes (e.g. Ford, BMW, Toyota) have completely different proprietary CAN DBC matrices, message frequencies, and arbitration IDs. A model trained on Kia Soul cannot detect intrusions on a Tesla or Ford without domain adaptation."*
- **Defense Strategy for Paper:**
  1. Explicitly bound the research scope: We are evaluating **within-model fleet FL** (e.g., an OEM managing an enterprise taxi fleet, commercial delivery vans, or municipal vehicles sharing the identical ECU platform) OR **in-vehicle domain gateway FL** (collaborative intrusion detection across independent zonal gateways/subnets within a modern zonal E/E architecture).
  2. Frame the Dirichlet Non-IID skew not as differing vehicle models, but as differing **operational and cyber-attack exposure regimes** across vehicles operating under different road conditions, driving cycles, and adversary targeting.

### Issue 2.2: Extreme Dataset Subsampling (2.85% of Raw Frames)
- **Vulnerability:** The raw HCRL dataset contains **17,558,346 CAN frames**. However, `02_preprocessing.ipynb` reads only the first 100,000 frames from each scenario (500,000 frames total, or 2.85%).
- **Reviewer Objection:** *"Why did the authors discard 97.15% of the available CAN data? In CAN bus intrusion detection, attacks such as DoS and Fuzzy injection exhibit long-term temporal bursts. Subsampling the first 100k frames may create an artificially easy or unrepresentative distribution."*
- **Defense Strategy for Paper:**
  1. Acknowledge the exact sample size transparently: 500,000 contiguous raw CAN frames yielding 31,250 non-overlapping 16-frame sequences ($W=16$).
  2. Justify via computational feasibility: Federated learning across 10 clients over 10 communication rounds with multi-seed repetitions requires training hundreds of decentralized epochs. 31,250 sequence windows provides substantial statistical power (4,690 test windows) while keeping GPU/CPU simulation tractable.
  3. Emphasize that chronological per-scenario splitting guarantees strict temporal causality and eliminates train-test leakage across time boundaries.

### Issue 2.3: Weakly Supervised Window Labeling Strategy
- **Vulnerability:** In `create_sliding_windows`, the parameter `label_mode="any"` is used. If a 16-frame window contains 15 normal frames and just 1 attack frame, the entire sequence is labeled as Attack ($y=1$).
- **Reviewer Objection:** *"Labeling an entire 16-frame window as an intrusion when only a single frame is anomalous creates severe label noise (weak supervision). An ECU IDS triggering an emergency response on a window that is 93.75% benign traffic could cause unacceptable vehicle disruption."*
- **Defense Strategy for Paper:**
  1. In safety-critical automotive systems, high recall is prioritized over precision: a single unauthorized high-priority frame (e.g. `0x000` bus takeover) must immediately trigger defense arbitration.
  2. Contrast with `label_mode="majority"` and acknowledge that window classification serves as a sequence-level anomaly detector, not a frame-level token tagger.

### Issue 2.4: Absence of Baseline Machine Learning Models
- **Vulnerability:** The repository only implements and benchmarks a single model: `CAN1DCNN`. There are no classical tabular ML models (Random Forest, XGBoost, Support Vector Machines) or recurrent architectures (GRU, LSTM, Transformer).
- **Reviewer Objection:** *"How do we know a 1D-CNN is necessary? CAN bus features are predominantly tabular (ID, DLC, 8 payload bytes). Could a simple Random Forest or Logistic Regression achieve 99% accuracy with a fraction of the computational complexity?"*
- **Defense Strategy for Paper:**
  1. Automotive edge constraints: 1D-CNN with GroupNorm has only 40,610 parameters (162.4 KB) and runs in **455.99 $\mu$s** on single CPU inference (well within the hard 1.0 ms real-time CAN deadline).
  2. Temporal sequence context: 16-frame sliding windows capture inter-arrival timing dynamics ($\Delta t$) and sequential bus transitions that stateless point-based ML models cannot model without handcrafted n-gram feature engineering.
  3. Action Item: Implement a lightweight Random Forest baseline on centralized features before submission to establish a defensible baseline comparison.

## Severity 3: Experimental Gaps & Artifact Inconsistencies

### Issue 3.1: Asymmetric Multi-Seed Evaluation Coverage
- **Description:** 
  - Multi-seed evaluations (Seeds 42, 43, 44) were conducted for the **9 robust federated experiments** (`results/metrics/robust_multiseed_summary.json`).
  - However, the **Centralized Baseline**, **IID FedAvg**, and **Non-IID FedAvg ($\alpha \in \{1.0, 0.5, 0.1\}$)** benchmarks were only executed on **Seed 42**!
- **Scientific Defect:** In the final paper results table, reporting Mean $\pm$ Std for Phase 3 while reporting single-seed numbers for Centralized, IID, and Non-IID creates an asymmetric, unscientific presentation.
- **Action Item:** Run seeds 43 and 44 for Centralized, IID, and Non-IID to produce complete, uniform Mean $\pm$ Std tables across all experimental regimes.

### Issue 3.2: Missing Visualization Generator in Notebook 05
- **Description:** The figure `results/figures/noniid_client_distributions.png` exists on disk (163 KB), and Notebook 05 asserts its existence in Cell 9. However, the cell that originally generated the figure was omitted or refactored into a print statement in Cell 4.
- **Scientific Impact:** A peer reviewer re-running `notebooks/05_federated_noniid.ipynb` from scratch would encounter an assertion error or fail to reproduce the figure.
- **Action Item:** Ensure the bar plot generation script is cleanly preserved in `scratch/` or as an executable script.

### Issue 3.3: Missing Recall Bar Chart for Attack B (Model-Update Corruption)
- **Description:** `results/figures/robust_per_attack_recall.png` plots per-attack-type detection accuracy across DoS, Fuzzy, Gear, and RPM specifically for **Attack A (Label Flipping)**. There is no corresponding figure for **Attack B (Model-Update Corruption)**.
- **Action Item:** Generate the companion figure for Model-Update Corruption to show per-attack detection collapse under FedAvg and robust recovery under Median and Trimmed Mean.

## Severity 4: Code & Documentation Cleanup

### Issue 4.1: Outdated Historical `AUDIT_REPORT.md`
- **Description:** `AUDIT_REPORT.md` at repository root documents an early Phase 1 audit from early October 2026. It reports that `src/data/loader.py` had a severe DLC parsing bug, `src/models/cnn1d.py` used BatchNorm, and `data/splits/` was empty. All of these defects were subsequent resolved during Phase 1 hardening and Phase 2 implementation.
- **Action Item:** Retain `AUDIT_REPORT.md` as historical project documentation, but ensure all reviewers and contributors reference `final_audit_2026/FINAL_AUDIT_REPORT.md` as the definitive state of truth.

### Issue 4.2: UndefinedMetricWarning during Pytest Execution
- **Description:** Running `pytest tests/` emits 3 warnings:
  `UndefinedMetricWarning: Only one class is present in y_true. ROC AUC score is not defined in that case.`
- **Cause:** In `tests/test_robust_aggregation.py`, synthetic test batches occasionally generate single-class batches where ROC-AUC cannot be computed.
- **Impact:** Harmless warning during unit tests, but should be suppressed with `zero_division=0` or explicit class count checks in metric calculations.
